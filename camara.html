T H E O R E T I C A L D I M E N S I O N O F T H E A R T I S T I C P R A C T I C E

Method of prosthesis, as if to say amen.

By method of prosthesis, I refer to a potentially unending artistic practice of self-limitation, one that concerns the significance of affirmation in general. Evolving over time, it structurally shapes the artworks, each time adding new morphological and semantic layers. The aesthetic qualities of the method derive from the mathematical operation of addition. The method of prosthesis, as a creative phenomenon, begins at the moment the mathematical variables are replaced by visual means. The shift of thought from the abstract to the en-formed turns the mathematical relation a + b = c into artistic practice.

Addition is thus approached as a dynamic equilibrium of parts of a like nature. Each part is analysed into new equivalent sums, revealing an inner tendency towards division. At the same time each part receives addition infinitely, so that the ever new sums produced reveal an inner tendency towards maximisation.

Addends and sum: Logos and Matter. To secure the necessary homogeneity of the relation, Logos is treated as a material phenomenon, without being deprived of its original meaning. Matter is treated as narrative action, without being deprived of its spatiality. They become visual phenomena of a like, intermediate nature, able to receive addition and division without disturbing the balance of the process.

Logos is divided. It becomes concept, cause, analogy, script1. It is divided further. It becomes word, rhythm, metre, form. It is divided further. New characters of the alphabet, new in form, are created. Spaces and punctuation marks are abolished. The characters receive a different material treatment and become autonomous. They cease to be auxiliary signs of a word and become signs of a wider visual phenomenon. The size of the scripts changes its dimensions, creating multiple scales of reading. Scripts hard to discern, yet never hard to understand, cease to be meaning alone. Logos is divided, densely described.

Matter is divided. It becomes space, field, quality, script. It is divided further. It becomes plane, point, line, form. It is divided further. Each material is presented in multiple stages of processing, so that the historicity of its process of formation is evident. Gypsum: rock, powder, liquid, at high temperatures, gypsum a solid material once more. The same happens with wax, with ink, with paper. Forms are born, linear, Platonic, angular, archaic, amorphous, on the plane and in space, at different stages of ripening. A narrative of materiality is composed in space. Liminal forms, yet never dematerialised, cease to be matter alone. Matter is divided, densely described.

In the early works anAglypto and lOgos, the first level of the method's influence becomes apparent. Each element of division stands in a continuous additive relation to the whole. Nothing is removed from the works, no text is deleted, no material is detached, every piece of information is recorded, every point is defined. The full overlays the void, in the way that zero is the neutral element of addition.

The works develop through continuous addition of materials, categorically excluding subtraction, thus suggesting an affirmation toward chance, error, and the unconscious dimension of creation. Not, however, in order to glorify these as they all end in themselves, but primarily to incorporate them into an immanent rational aesthetic.

The second level of the method's influence appears primarily in the work in nOmine DEI. This work develops as a colorless, larger-scale replica laid upon the pre-existing work lOgos, the latter providing the former with its spatial point of departure. The new work is thus structured on the basis of the older one, added to it in a particular way. At the same time, the older work acquires a new property: it becomes the matrix of the new work.

The same practice is followed throughout. The works contain the previous ones and foreshadow those to come. The additive practice thus affirms a new type of relation: the works relinquish part of their autonomy without forfeiting it. They are elevated additively, thus forming a whole at once unified and autonomous.

In the installation saturAted phenOmena facAdes2, an additional aspect of the second level of the additive method's influence becomes visible. The installation relates to the works not materially but primarily conceptually. It consists of the composition of dozens of handmade books digitally processed, oriented in two temporal directions. It functions as an orienting device – genealogical (past) and interpretive (future) – for the preceding works. It reveals to the viewer the sites of origin of the forms and meanings of the works, while simultaneously proposing contemporary interpretive paths. The works undergo additive elevation3, relinquishing part of their autonomy without forfeiting it.
The third level of the method's influence is manifested in the installation as a whole. The installation amen: a Logos of signs for all that are not is structured on the basis of the earlier amen: for all that are not. It is added to it in a particular manner. At the same time, the earlier installation acquires a new property: it becomes the matrix of the new one. The installation contains the previous one and foreshadows the next.

The same practice is followed throughout. The installations contain the previous ones and foreshadow those to come. The additive practice thus affirms a new type of relation: the installation relinquishes part of its autonomy without forfeiting it. It is elevated additively, thus forming a whole at once unified and autonomous.

A mode of connection with the world is thus proposed: a yielding that does not result in loss; a present that retains the past and acquires the future4; a receptivity to becoming; a thus-far so be it: Amen.

Spatial Dimensions of all that are not.

By all that are not, I refer to the phenomena that are not according to the traditional mode of being in the world. Those we recognise empirically in a secret and paradoxical manner. Those which Art, by privilege, can not only thematise but also give form to.

Through them I propose a temporary turning of the gaze away from the settled certainty indicated by the physical presence of objects, and its turn towards doubtful, absent and uncertain phenomena which have nevertheless shaped the consciousness of civilisations across the centuries.

For the Greeks, meaning (noēma) is definition; it is a boundary. It is not that at which something stops, but that from which something begins its essence, as Heidegger says5. In what way, then, are those things that contain meaning yet lack a compact definition?

We say that the point is, when it signifies. The point exists as the end of every sentence and as a mark distinguishing other elements of language. It exists for Euclid as that which has no parts, in analytic geometry as part of a straight line, and in the culture of the Aborigines as a topographical sacred site. It exists as a self-subsistent wonder and as a relation to the signified. It exists at once as the principle and cause of the world; it exists outside it too. It exists as an autonomous visual stimulus and as ideal, dimensionless matter. It exists as a condensed circle, it exists as a field, it exists as a sphere.

Since the point signifies manifoldly and contradictorily, its fundamental property of signification is reasonably called into question. When the point does not signify, it cannot be a point; and while it truly exists, it truly is not.

Likewise irrational numbers, the circle, the infinite: are not. The cross is not. Prayer, miracle, curse, prophecy: are not. Illusion, space, dream, time, revelation, logos, love: are not.

If I wish for phenomena of this kind to exist visually in a manner proportionate to them, the search for and articulation of an additional space of material reality emerges as a necessity.

Such an inquiry becomes manifest primarily in the works in nOmine DEI and apocAlypsis. Each sheet in these works constitutes a visual unit of double frontality. It was worked in such a way that one side presupposes the other. Its imperceptible thickness was treated as an additional dimension, as a field for the penetration and intermingling of materials and meanings – as an active field of interaction of visual discourse.

Thus the invisible space – the formless, the insignificant for many – became the most decisive formative agent of the works. A hidden space was substantiated. It became the surplus of spatial dimensions, the unifying element that transforms 2-dimensional surfaces into a 3-dimensional object. An additional space for all that are not.

Temporal Dimensions of Forms.

The visual point of departure of the works is the point and its geometric prospect, the line. Point and line initially take form as fields, arrangements, and shapes, and subsequently as symbols, scripts, and images bearing transhistorical cultural reference.
The sun, the moon, the cross, the spiral, the Platonic solids, mathematical sequences, natural laws, optical illusions, the Testament, the Apocalypse, erotic poetry, philosophical texts, symbols of alchemy, seals and sacred objects are rendered visually anew.

Yet the transhistoricity they carry makes their genealogical erosion impossible. Still retaining their emotional and noetic influence both socially and individually, they coexist actively with contemporary aesthetic forms. New forms of forms emerge. That which once appeared as belonging to the past, through contemporary artistic practice, is transposed into the future, while simultaneously preserving its morphological point of departure.

Across civilisations the sun has symbolised the generative cause of life. In the works, the sun remains a circle; it remains the evolution of the point; it remains a principle of the world. Yet contemporary conceptions of the origin of the world propose another point, another circle, another sun. The sun remains another sun. The solar circle opens itself within the horizon of temporality6. The sun remains another sun of every world.

In the works, each letter remains script; it remains the evolution of the line; it remains the origin of logos. Yet contemporary conceptions of the origin of logos propose as beginnings image-lines, symbolic-lines, unwritten-lines, other scripts. The line remains another script. Script as line unfolds within the horizon of temporality. Script remains another script of every world.
