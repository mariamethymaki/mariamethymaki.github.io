Instead of CV, a text from the work: in nOmine DEI: 60 scUlpted pOems (f.27)

A Diploma from the School of Architecture, National Technical University of Athens. Postgraduate level. In other words: the full initiation.

We dove into the inner structure of the natural world, training our static intuition so our power to compose could reach the far edge of precision. We could give form to anything – real, imagined – through drawings buildable, measurable, aesthetically flawless. Thesis grade: ten, with honors. Doctoral level, the committee confessed in unison. In other words: I had mastered it.

Except for one course. Architectural Design of Paradise. That was the one I needed – to study its structure, to see if I could rebuild the one I had lost. I didn't even know what tools it required: bioclimatic strategies? urban survey methods? territorial planning? Martin once tried to say something relevant, but we were far too left for him. So I went on.

A degree from the School of Fine Arts, Sculpture. Postgraduate again. In other words: another planet. Handmade. Personal. Free.

But I got confused for a while. Every attempt to give visual form to an inner necessity was praised as a potentially fascinating paradise. Every human being, a paradise.

They never told us what the trees looked like – the ones we hid our nakedness behind. They never told us how the flowers of our silence smelled. Not a word about which color made our togetherness come alive. Whether the mountains had roots of reinforced concrete, or whether we just mixed red, yellow, and blue so they might hold – I never found out.

I just built a wall and in a notebook I wrote, so the years of exile wouldn't go to waste.

Until I melted. Not me – my life melted. Which is to say: I melted the way my life melted. I found it all spilled on the ground. Luckily, I knew how to patch things together. And I happened to have a spatula at home – and warmth in my land. I got back to work. A bit of hand. A bit of foot. A bit of torso.

I had never made anything so ugly. In other words: a terrible work. In other words: I was saved.

In other words — amen.
