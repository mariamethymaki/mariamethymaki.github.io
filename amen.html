is the title I give to this large-scale installation, for those things that do not exist in the traditional sense of being. It both contains and is contained by the installations I have already created and those I am yet to create. It contains and is contained in three parts; it is at once a whole and a part of a threefold totality.

Each part, while maintaining its visual autonomy, simultaneously implies, indicates, and conceptually and morphologically presupposes the other parts and the whole, as both a sum of parts and a unified entity. Thus, in speaking of a part, we are already to think of the same, of the other two parts, and of the unity of the one.

At the same time, each sub-part of each part, while preserving its visual autonomy, likewise implies, indicates, conceptually and morphologically presupposes the other sub-parts, the other parts, and the whole, as both a multiplicity and a unity. Thus, in referring to a part of partial, we already think of the same, the other partialities, and the other parts, while contemplating the unity of the one.

Thus, in saying book, we indicate an autonomous work of art, while already thinking of the totality of books and contemplating the unity of the installation – that is, the whole as both a set of members and a unified entity. Handmade books and digitized books, for those things that are not. Books concerning doubtful, absent, and uncertain phenomena which, nevertheless, have shaped the consciousness of civilizations across the centuries.

According to the established certainty suggested by rational thought and the spatial presence of objects, the point does not exist. Irrational numbers, the circle, the infinite do not exist. The cross does not exist. Prayer, curse, prophecy do not exist. Illusion, space, dream, time, revelation, speech, love do not exist.

And yet, in another way, they do exist.

Thus the question is implicitly posed: what is and what is not? At the same time, the further question is implied: what is Being itself? Not in order to be answered literally, but to prompt artistic creation to approach it through its own means, and to offer visitors the possibility of composing a personal cosmology through inquiry.
